A Pittsburgh university opened a robotics laboratory on September 19. The lab received a $2 million research grant to develop navigation tools for people who use wheelchairs. University engineers and students will build a prototype that combines cameras and distance sensors to detect obstacles in campus buildings.

The team plans to recruit 30 adult volunteers for supervised trials beginning in November. Participation is free, and every volunteer must give informed consent. Researchers will measure collision avoidance, travel time, and ease of use. A safety officer will supervise each trial and can stop a test at any time.

Project leader Maya Chen said the equipment is experimental and is not yet approved for use outside the laboratory. The university will hold a public workshop in October to explain the research and collect feedback from disability advocacy groups. The grant covers a two-year study, and the team has not yet published results.
